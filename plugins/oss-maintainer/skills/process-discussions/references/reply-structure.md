# Reply structure and honesty rules

Order that works, per thread:

1. Open warm and specific. Acknowledge genuinely good behaviour: fresh framing, a working
   prototype, self-qualification, mockups. Warmth comes from specificity, never from
   compliments about the question itself.
2. **Mirror the decomposition back, numbered**, marking verified facts as verified ("I
   checked the code: …").
3. When the use case is ambiguous, or the decision owner needed it explained, **play it back
   as a concrete worked example** ("Let me play this back, correct me where I get it wrong:
   in March you have twelve papers…").
4. Route each need to its home with links: canonical threads, upstream issues, decision
   records.
5. Questions tailored to what actually shapes the design, not generic ones.
6. Invitations matched to what the author offered: testers get test asks, implementers get
   building invites, designers get design questions.
7. Close with `Status: **<outcome>**` plus a one-line summary of the routing.
8. When the answer is **no**, say it in the first paragraph with the reasons (the runtime is
   about to change, the estimate does not hold for local models). Acknowledge real work
   without letting it change the answer.

Honesty rules: no feature promises, no timelines ("no commitment on timing yet"); constraints
stated with their reasons and their public anchor; "the door is deliberately kept open"
beats false enthusiasm and beats silence.

Style rules: no marketing filler or throat-clearing (never "Fair question, and it deserves a
straight answer", never "Great idea!"). Open by *answering*. Attribution line only when
`[comms].agent_attribution = "disclaimer"`.
